**Social meanings**, the ideologically mediated relationships between linguistic features and stances, identities, and/or personal characteristics, are the "glue" that connects linguistic variation and social processes.
As such, understanding the many dimensions of social meanings is fundamental to language variation and change.
I investigate social meanings through perception methods.

My dissertation research {% cite villarreal_construction_2018 --prefix s-m %} found that Californian listeners perceive features of the [California Vowel Shift](https://doi.org/10.1215/00031283-1599950) as Californian, sounding like a Valley girl, and confident, but not some of the other attributes that [past](https://doi.org/10.1177/13670069080120010301) [studies](https://doi.org/10.1215/00031283-1277501) have argued is associated with these vowels.

I followed up on this research with Mary Kohn to investigate Kansans' perceptions of backed <span class="small-caps">[trap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexical_set#Standard_lexical_sets_for_English)</span>, a vowel feature ideologically associated with California but found in both California and Kansas.
We found that young Kansan listeners were aware of an association between California and backed <span class="small-caps">trap</span>, but when assessed with a larger array of traits, they heard backed <span class="small-caps">trap</span> as generally prestigious and not as particularly Californian or Kansan;
this finding brings us closer to understanding how supralocal features can spread {% cite villarreal_local_2021 --prefix s-m %}.
